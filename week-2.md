Week 2: Preparing Your Own Record

Section One: Nephite Records

What blessings did the Nephites enjoy because these records were kept?

Because Lehi and Nephi obtained the Brass Plates from Laban (1 Nephi 4:13–17), the Nephites did not lose their faith or forget God. The Lord told Nephi that it is better for one man to die than for a whole nation to dwindle and perish in unbelief.

Because these records were preserved, the Nephites enjoyed key blessings:

Keeping their faith: They knew the commandments, understood the law of Moses, and learned about Jesus Christ.

Keeping their language: Unlike the people of Zarahemla, who lost their language because they had no records (Omni 1:15–17), the Nephites could read, write, and teach their children.

Guidance and repentance: When the people struggled or made bad choices, prophets used the plates to call them to repent and remind them of God's promises.

What blessings do you enjoy because the Nephites kept these records, and Joseph Smith brought them forth to our people?

In 2 Nephi 28:2, we learn that the words from this book are of "great worth." Because of the Nephite records and the work of Joseph Smith:

A clear testimony of Christ: I have a second witness that Jesus Christ lives, loves us, and is our Savior.

Help for daily trials: The stories of prophets like Nephi and Alma teach me how to pray, trust God, and make good choices.

Peace and hope: The Book of Mormon reminds me that God keeps His promises to His children today, which brings peace when life is hard.

Section Two: Creating Your Goal

My Five-Year Goal

In the next five years, I want to graduate from college, start a stable career, and build good financial and spiritual habits to support my family and serve in the Church.

1. Discover

What I need to work on: I need better daily study habits, improved time management, and a realistic budget.

Spiritual need: I need to seek the Lord's help each morning so I do not feel overwhelmed by school and daily work.

2. Plan

Education: Take 12 to 14 credit hours each semester, complete assignments on time, and visit tutors or teachers when I need help.

Finances: Create a monthly budget, pay a full tithe, avoid unnecessary debt, and put a set amount into savings every month.

Spiritual: Read scriptures and pray for 15 minutes each morning before checking my phone or social media.

3. Act

Wake up at 6:30 AM on weekdays to pray, read scriptures, and plan the day.

Dedicate two focused hours every evening to homework with notifications turned off.

Pay tithing first upon receiving income, then transfer money to savings.

Pray daily in faith for focus, peace, and help during exams.

4. Reflect

Weekly review: Every Sunday, review my calendar, grades, and budget to see what went well and where I can improve.

Semester review: At the end of each semester, evaluate my overall progress, thank Heavenly Father in prayer, and adjust my routine for the next term.

Section Three: Refining Your Personal Goal

Steps to Improve Personal Record-Keeping

Keep a simple weekly journal: Spend 10 minutes every Sunday evening writing down key events, lessons learned, and prayers that were answered.

Maintain a digital goal tracker: Use a clean note or document on my phone or computer with monthly goals, checking them off on the first day of each month.

Record promptings immediately: Keep a quick-access note on my phone to write down spiritual impressions or scripture insights before I forget them.

Section Four: Personal Reflection Paragraph

Writing down my five-year goal and breaking it into small steps is an essential part of keeping a record of my life. A goal kept only in my head is easy to forget, but writing it down turns it into a clear plan I can follow. Just as the Nephites were blessed because they kept a record of God's hand in their lives, keeping track of my own goals and spiritual growth will bless my posterity. My future children and grandchildren will be able to see that I had real challenges, set righteous goals, and relied on the Lord to overcome them. Writing my goals also allows me to pray over them and make sure they match Heavenly Father's plan for me. Putting a pen to paper or typing out a goal is an act of faith—it shows that I believe my life has purpose, and it creates a lasting record of how the Lord leads me step by step.
